\begin{abstract}
Event cameras have the potential to revolutionize vision systems with their high temporal resolution and dynamic range, yet they remain susceptible to lens flare, a fundamental optical artifact that causes severe degradation. In event streams, flare forms a complex, spatio-temporal distortion that has been largely overlooked. We present \ours, the first systematic framework for removing lens flare from event camera data. We first establish the theoretical foundation by deriving a physics-grounded forward model of the non-linear suppression mechanism. This insight enables the creation of the \textbf{E-Deflare Benchmark}, a resource featuring a large-scale simulated training set, \textbf{E-Flare2.7K}, and the first paired real-world test set, \textbf{E-Flare-R}, captured by our optical system. Using \textbf{E-DeflareNet}, a standard 3D U-Net, we show that a model trained on our physically grounded data outperforms existing event filters. Experiments validate our approach and show clear benefits for downstream tasks. Code and data samples are in the supplement.

\end{abstract}
